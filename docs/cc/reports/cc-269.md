## The proposal for Craig (verbatim from the audit §7)

**A fifth WAF custom rule, in the free slot.**

- **Name:** `AI search crawlers (FIX-1390)`
- **Expression:** `(http.user_agent contains "ExaSearchBot") or (http.user_agent contains "KeenableBot")`
- **Action:** **Managed Challenge**, never Block. A crawler cannot solve it and
  a browser can, so a mis-match costs a human one interstitial, not a page.
- **Slot:** the **5th, currently empty** (§5: the live entrypoint holds 4). Put
  it after `Probe skip (FIX-1057)`. Do not merge it into `Meta`: `Meta`'s action
  is Block, and merging would either block Exa outright or soften Meta.
  *If `Verified bots skip (FIX-1305)` is later re-added to this phase, the
  zone is at 5 of 5. That is the moment to fold `Meta` into this rule as a third
  `or` and accept a challenge for meta-webindexer instead of a block.*
- **What it saves:** 4,634 checks/day, so **−19.1k commands/day** (about −65 %)
  and **−4.6k origin renders/day** (about −30 %). Spend falls to ~9.9k/day, and
  month-end is ≈ 452k even with no hold.
- **What it costs:** Exa's and Keenable's indexes stop refreshing civic pages.
  That is a reach call, so it is Craig's. Googlebot, Bingbot and OAI-SearchBot
  are untouched.
- **The toggle alternative** (AI Crawl Control → block AI crawlers): Cloudflare
  files Exa, Keenable and OAI-SearchBot under **Search Engine Crawler**, not "AI
  Crawler". So the toggle is **not expected to stop the two that spend**. Craig
  can confirm in the dashboard which bots it lists.
- `cf.verified_bot_category eq "Search Engine Crawler"` is rejected, because it
  also catches Google and Bing.
- Fields: none of `http.user_agent` / `http.request.uri.path` / `cf.client.bot` /
  `cf.verified_bot_category` carries a plan label in Cloudflare's fields
  reference, which marks restricted fields per entry ("Enterprise add-on", "Pro
  or above", …). All four are Free-plan fields.
