## The census — `docs/audits/2026-11-28-members-with-no-officials-row.md`

**1. How many.** At least **185** distinct members have no row:

- 117th: 172 (151 House, 21 Senate)
- 118th: 93 (76 House, 17 Senate)
- 119th: 10 (7 House, 3 Senate)

A member counts once in each Congress they sat in. All 185 are `why: "none"`.
**None is `many`**, and no bioguide sits on two of the 542 bioguide-keyed rows
(539 elected and active, 3 former). Together they hold 6,461 unresolved
cosponsor appearances on 675 of the 1,516 stamped measures. All 114 unresolved
sponsors (257 measures) are among them. 185 is a floor: the 117th House is
short about 171 members a roll and the 118th about 111, against read 3's 151
and 76. 136 of the 185 already have FEC `candidate` rows under their name (164
rows), and 49 have no row of any kind.

**2. The 117th and 118th share.** In the House, 265,206 of the 286,795 missing
member votes are in the 117th and 118th, **92.5 %**:

| House | missing member votes | of official casts |
|---|---|---|
| 117th | 156,512 | 393,142 |
| 118th | 108,694 | 425,633 |
| 119th | 21,589 | 294,850 |

The 119th did not fit "one cause". Its mean shortfall is 31.7 a roll, but its
median is about 4. One more read found the reason: 54 rolls,
`2026-house-156` … `-209` (05-12 → 06-08), store 98–193 rows each and carry
17,017 of the 21,589. They are a truncated write and not missing members
(FIX-1465). Without them, the 117th–118th share is **98.3 %**. On the Senate
side, prod cannot answer yet: the only rolls with all four counts are the
119th's 110 detailed ones, short 0 of 10,998. The local clone's yea / nay
comparison puts 95.5 % of the Senate gap in the 117th and 118th.

**3. The code that would create the rows.** `officials.ts` matches by bioguide
in memory, then updates or inserts. Every row it writes is `is_active: true`
and `tier: 'elected'`, with `term_end` hard-coded to `2027-01-03` for a
Representative. After the loop, its reconcile step takes the roster it was
passed as "the feed". So feeding it `/member/congress/117` (557 members, above
the 500 floor) would do three things: write 557 rows as sitting members, run
promotion against them, and flip every sitting member who was not in the
117th to `former`. A backfill needs its own writer or a mode that is not "the
current roster". Read 6's items are the shape `officials.ts` already parses.

**4. The name-keyed resolvers a former row would enter.** These have no
`is_active` or `tier` filter:

- **irs990 officers** (`writer.ts:257-305`): last write wins on the
  first + last key. A former namesake whose id sorts later takes the match at
  confidence 1.0.
- **plum-book** (`index.ts:422-464`): first wins, in unordered return order.
  The matched row's `is_active` is then set from the appointee.
- **LittleSis** (`matcher.ts`): surname pools. A lone surname binds with no
  first-name check.
- **agency-leadership**: the nominee lookup refuses two or more matches, then
  inserts a new row; an adopted row is written `is_active: true`. The Wikidata
  recovery fails on two or more.

fec-bulk's two name matchers, the Senate vote resolver and promotion all
filter on `is_active`, so a former row stays out of them. The Senate vote
resolver also filters on `tier = 'elected'`. That cuts both ways: it is safe,
but no former senator's 117th–118th vote can land until it changes. The one
known namesake among the 185 is exactly the risky case. Sen. Robert Menendez
(M000639, NJ) has no row. Row `747c96d0…`, "Robert Menendez", is the sitting
Rep. Rob Menendez, also NJ.
