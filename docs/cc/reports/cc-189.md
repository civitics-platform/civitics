## 2. The landing

### `crawl_gate` (+139 / −0 against the prod body)

Rebuilt from prod's own `pg_get_functiondef` with anchored insertions: ten DECLAREs after `v_age_min`, and the `yield_to` block immediately before `-- ── blackout`. Order is now: backoff → peer_backoff → **peer_running → peer_due** → blackout → cycle_cooldown → clear.

- **Entry parse.** An entry is `{job, pipeline, lead_s}`. A malformed entry warns and is ignored: not an object, no job or pipeline, or a `lead_s` that is non-numeric or ≤ 0. So is a `job` absent from `cron.job`, and a non-array `yield_to`. All fail OPEN.
- **`peer_running`.** The peer's own `data_sync_log` row (`status = 'running'`, younger than 6 h) is checked first. Then one `cron.job_run_details` scan covers every listed job (`running`, younger than 6 h).
- **`peer_due`.** `due := cron_next_daily(schedule, now − 60 s)`; due when `due − now ≤ lead_s` and `now − due < 60 s`. A **parked** job (`active = false`) never fires, so it is never due. A non-daily schedule warns and can only ever be `peer_running`.

### `cron_next_daily(p_schedule, p_now)` (new)

STABLE, INVOKER, `search_path public, pg_catalog`. The lifted parser, plus a range guard: minute > 59 or hour > 23 → NULL. `prod_op_gate` keeps its own copy and was not touched. Read back on prod at 02:28:13Z: `('0 6 * * *', now())` → `2026-10-04 06:00:00+00`, `('30 6 * * *', now())` → `06:30:00+00`, `('*/15 * * * *', now())` → NULL.

### `peer_wait_gate(p_pipelines, p_wait_max_s)` (new)

`box_backoff_gate`'s shape. It polls every 60 s while the EC inflight key exists or a `running` row younger than 6 h exists for a listed pipeline. It returns `{clear, waited_s, wait_max_s, polls, blocking}`. `civitics.peer_gate_wait_max_s` clamps to 0–3,600. INVOKER, no COMMIT.

## 4. What contradicted the design or the prompt

1. **The FE crawl's pipeline is `financial_entity_totals_refresh`**, not `fe_totals_refresh` as B5 said. B5 also said to read the writer, so the build uses the real name. With the wrong name the daily's belt would never have seen an FE unit.

2. **`fe_crawl.blackout` is `[]` on prod; the blackout never covered fe-crawl.** The design's "the blackout has held since 08-29" is true of ec-crawl only. fe-crawl overlapped the 06:00 daily and the 06:30 taggers on **every** one of the 8 mornings measured. Those were caught-up no-op units (0.1–0.5 s), so no harm so far. The day the FE crawl has a real slice at 06:00 is the 08-29 shape. Part 1 is the FE crawl's first protection of any kind in that window.

3. **The same-instant race, and the 60 s grace (the one departure from B2).**
   - **The race.** The crawl and its peer fire in the same ~0.3 s. The peer's `running` row is committed only after its preamble (advisory lock, `prod_session_state()`, and the daily's VM read). A `peer_running` check at the crawl's start therefore cannot be relied on to see it.
   - **Where B2's arithmetic leaves the hole.** B2's `cron_next_daily(schedule, now) − now ≤ lead_s` stops being true the instant a slot passes. At 06:00 the tagger's lead window happens to cover the gap. At **06:30**, the last peer's slot, nothing does.
   - **What that would have meant.** Without the grace, fe-crawl at 06:30:00 (and ec-crawl at 06:30) would open a unit beside the taggers every morning, and the receipt week's "overlap count 0 every day" would fail by construction.
   - **The fix.** `peer_due` also holds for a peer that fired under 60 s ago. B7's 06:31Z cell still reads `clear` (tested: fired 2 min ago → `clear`). The grace is a constant, `c_peer_grace_s`, not config.
