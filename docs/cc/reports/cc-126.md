## 9. What contradicted the design

**Five things, and the first two changed what shipped.**

**1. The orphan rows are not partial, and FIX-1184's bullet is wrong about the
whole mechanism.** The bullet proposes a terminal "abandoned" status so Guard 1
can tell abandoned from running, which presupposes the rows hold prefixes. They
hold *complete* key sets — and the two cycle-2024 rows from consecutive nights
hold byte-identical counts, which is not a coincidence you can read two ways.
The prompt was right to make item 4's shape conditional on read 3 rather than on
the bullet. Had I built the bullet's design I would have shipped a status column
to distinguish two states, one of which does not occur, and left the actual
defect — that the pas2 arm is never stamped for cycles other than the one whose
*indiv* completed — in place.

**2. FIX-1182's motivating example points the opposite way from the conclusion
it motivates.** The bullet's strongest argument against the apply is "it would
delete a sitting senator's $6.03M". That money is on `c6825cc2`, role_title
`Candidate for Senator` — a duplicate. The sitting senator is `3e559d5c` and
every one of her 5,243 rows was emitted on 09-13. So for this recipient the
residue is *exactly* the double-count FIX-1106 exists to remove, and the example
argues for the apply rather than against it. The bullet also hypothesises the
FIX-937 gate as the cause ("128 officials receiving zero emitted rows is the
shape a gate produces"); her `role_title` is in the pool, and 122 of the 128 pass
that gate. A gate *is* implicated, but it is the FIX-1025 office-prefix
companion, and it accounts for 5 officials, not 128.

Worth naming the generalisable bit: cc-119 reached its conclusion from
`last_write` clustering and one hand-picked name. The name was the wrong row of
two with the same CAND_ID, and the clustering is what a re-binding looks like
too. One keyed query over the whole cohort — does anyone else hold this
CAND_ID, and were they emitted to — was cheap and settled 88 of 128 at once. The
cohort question was available the whole time.

**3. The prompt's read-5 script list names the wrong fourth script.** It lists
`remediate-bound-cross-person.ts` as one of the three siblings that print. That
script does bulk-rewrite `financial_relationships`, but it never calls
`drainFrRewrite`, `declareDrainTail` or `printTailTable` — it carries its own
hand-rolled tail and its own inline VACUUM. The actual fourth `drainFrRewrite`
caller is `merge-same-person-official-dupes.ts`, which is also the third entry
in the existing structural test's `SCRIPTS` list. I built against the real set
and made the test derive it, so the question cannot be got wrong again.

**4. The "all four print before their go-ahead point" assertion cannot anchor to
the first `BEGIN`.** My first attempt did, and `merge-same-person-official-dupes`
failed it legitimately: it opens and commits an unrelated step-0 bookkeeping
transaction at line 1634, hundreds of lines before the manifest is built. The
assertion anchors to the **last** `BEGIN` (the manifest transaction) plus every
`if (!apply)` return. Noting it because "print before the first write" is the
