## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The 2026-08-10 12:00 UTC firing spent **99.2% of its 2h36m window before it
executed a single unit of rollup work.** Its one and only chunk took **75
seconds**; `elapsed_seconds` was **9,367**. The missing ~9,290 s went into the
dirty-set build — the `SELECT DISTINCT fr.to_id … WHERE updated_at > watermark`

FIX-1002 guards. On a quiet, freshly-restarted prod that same query measures
**59.07 s with 156,792 buffer reads and 4 buffer hits** (99.997% cache miss,
~1.2 GB of cold random I/O). On 08-10 it ran concurrently with two six-hour
`entity_connections` rebuilds that were evicting the 256 MB buffer pool, and it
degraded ~157×. The recipient it then spent its single chunk on was
`NATIONAL ASSOCIATION OF CONVENIENCE STORES POLITICAL ACTION COMMITTEE` — a PAC,
**not an official**, because the dirty-set predicate has no `to_type = 'official'`
filter and 1,640 of the 2,890 "recipients" are `financial_entities`.

## 3. The measurement that settles it

### 3a. Where the 2h36m went

`data_sync_log` metadata for the 08-10 12:00 firing, verbatim:

```json
{ "mode": "incremental",     "chunks": 1,             "recipients_done": 1,
  "rollup_rows": 201,        "stop_reason": "budget_exhausted",
  "dirty_recipients": 2889,  "dirty_weight": 4587430,
  "budget_seconds": 7200,    "elapsed_seconds": 9367,
  "slowest_chunk_seconds": 75,
  "rows_per_second_seed": 30.00, "rows_per_second": 19.985,
  "chunk_target_seconds": 300, "resume_at_chunk": 2 }
```

`chunks: 1` and `slowest_chunk_seconds: 75` mean **total chunk time = 75 s**.
`v_started` is stamped `clock_timestamp()` at procedure entry, and `elapsed` is
read at the top of each loop iteration. So **9,292 of 9,367 seconds elapsed
before chunk 1 finished, and 75 of them were chunk 1.**

The only substantial work between procedure entry and the loop is the watermark
capture (`MAX(updated_at)` — an index-scan-backward `Limit`, cost 1.62, free) and
**the dirty-set build**.

### 3b. The dirty-set build, measured on a quiet box

`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` of the exact FIX-1002 query with the live watermark
(`2026-08-08 22:59:39.694444+00`), run at 02:05 UTC with `pg_stat_activity`
showing zero other non-idle backends and 57 minutes of uptime:

```
Aggregate (actual time=59071.741..59071.746 rows=1 loops=1)
  Buffers: shared hit=4 read=156792
  -> HashAggregate (actual time=59063.180..59063.724 rows=2890)
     -> Bitmap Heap Scan on financial_relationships (actual time=33811.953..58928.591 rows=181616)
          Heap Blocks: exact=78525
          Buffers: shared hit=4 read=156675
        -> BitmapAnd (actual time=33793.313..33793.315)
           -> Bitmap Index Scan on financial_relationships_updated_at
                (actual time=131.748..131.748 rows=181717)   Buffers: read=234
           -> Bitmap Index Scan on financial_relationships_donor_rollup_idx
                (actual time=33650.094..33650.094 rows=6628447) Buffers: read=77918
Execution Time: 59072.365 ms
```

Four numbers carry the whole finding:

1. **59.07 s on an idle box.** Against a 7,200 s budget that is 0.8% — survivable.
   Under 08-10's contention it became ~9,290 s, **~157×**, which is 129% of the
   budget. Playbook **C4**: runtime measurements are floors.
2. **`hit=4`, `read=156,792`.** A 99.997% cache miss, ~1.2 GB of cold random I/O,
   against `shared_buffers = 256 MB`. This query has no cache residency by
   construction, so it is maximally exposed to anything else evicting the pool.
3. **The `donor_rollup_idx` leg costs 33.65 s and reads 6,628,447 index entries
   to contribute nothing.** That index is `(to_id, relationship_type, from_id)`;
   `relationship_type` is not the leading column, so the planner full-scans a
   609 MB index to build one side of a `BitmapAnd`. The `updated_at` leg alone
   returns 181,717 rows in **131 ms**. The AND "saves" heap blocks the scan reads
   anyway (78,525 exact). It is a planner-legal choice made possible by an index
   gap, and it is **57% of the query's runtime**.
4. **This is outside FIX-1002's guard.** FIX-1002 armed the guard from chunk 1
   (correctly — see playbook C3's addendum). The cost simply moved one level up,
   to work that happens before iteration 1 exists. Same blind spot, new location.

Current dirty-set cost distribution (`donor_count`, 2,890 recipients):
`min 1 · p50 525 · p90 4,042 · p99 17,639 · max 81,215`, 1,710 with no odt row.
Playbook **D8** — and note the p50 of 525 is 15× the 36 quoted in FIX-1002's
census, because that census was over *all* officials and this is over the

## 4. What actually changed between 08-09 and 08-10

Four things, in order of contribution.

**(i) FIX-1002 went live on prod in that exact gap.** The 08-09 12:00 run's
metadata carries `budget_seconds: 16200` and `chunk_size: 50` (the FIX-972
procedure). The 08-10 12:00 run carries `budget_seconds: 7200`, `dirty_weight`
and `stop_reason` (the FIX-1002 procedure). The dirty-set build is inherited
unchanged from FIX-972 — but the budget dropped **4.5 h → 2 h**, so a pre-loop
cost that used to consume 55% of the window now consumes 129% of it.

**(ii) ~171,491 FR rows were touched on 08-09** by two `fec_bulk` runs
(15:55→16:55 and 22:28→23:43) — the first full-volume FEC writes since the
FIX-998 hold lift. This is what made the dirty window 181,717 FR rows / 2,890
recipients / 4,589,368 weight, i.e. a dirty set whose weight **exceeds the entire
4,088,218-row donation corpus** in FIX-1002's own census. It is also what pushed
`financial_relationships` to 77.1% all-visible with no vacuum owner to fix it.

**(iii) 08-10 was a Monday, and Monday is the collision.** `rebuild-ec-incremental-mon`
(jobid 22, `0 8 * * 1`) ran **08:00 → 14:13, 22,379.5 s, killed by the 6 h
statement_timeout**. `entity-connection-stats-rebuild` (jobid 16, `0 11 * * 1,3`)
ran **11:00 → 17:00, 21,610.9 s, same axe**. The 12:00 donor-rollup ran entirely
inside the overlap of both. This recurs: 08-03, also a Monday, shows the identical
pair (EC-mon 6 h → killed; ECS 4h09m). Both are still stranded `running` in
`data_sync_log` — FIX-990, now two rows not one.

**(iv) The six FIX-1003 arm vacuums never fired.** 0 of 12, all startup timeouts,
including the 11:05–11:18 slot that sits between the two donor-rollup firings by
design.

Contrast with the healthy run the prompt anchors on: **08-09 09:00, 247 dirty
recipients, 5 chunks, 1,422.9 s, `complete`, slowest chunk 364 s.** That was the
morning after `ec-`/`fe-`/`dpr-vacuum-analyze` all fired successfully at 08-09
02:00 (Sunday) and before the FEC ingest. It is a real datapoint about vacuum
state mattering — and it is also the only fast run in the window. The 08-06/07/08
firings all took **3h04m–3h30m** and all logged `partial`. The steady state was
already bad; 08-10 is where it went to zero.

## 5. Decision gate

**(A) Regression / instance-wide episode — YES, dominant.** But the mechanism is
not either of the two the prompt proposed. It is not primarily "FR degraded by
FEC writes" (that is contributor ii, real but not sufficient — the same query
costs 59 s on a quiet box *with FR still at 77.1% AV*), and it is not primarily
"arm-vacuum jobs not firing" (contributor iv, real, and separately serious). It
is: **an unguarded, unindexed dirty-set build sitting outside FIX-1002's chunk
loop, whose 59-s idle cost multiplies ~157× under the Monday double-rebuild
contention, against a budget FIX-1002 had just cut to 2 h.**

**(B) FIX-1002's bound holding as designed — PARTLY, and the inference the prompt
draws from it does not follow.** The bound *did* work: the run stopped at chunk 2
with `stop_reason: budget_exhausted`, committed its one chunk durably, and did
**not** produce a second 6 h blowout like 08-08's run 195. That is FIX-1002
succeeding. But "2 of 2,889" does **not** mean per-recipient cost was in historic
range and the budget was correctly refusing a large dirty set. Per-*chunk* cost
was **75 seconds**. The rollup work was fine.

This matters because it changes the FIX-1004 recommendation. At the bulk regime's
measured 0.102 s/recipient, 2,889 recipients is ~5 minutes of chunk work — but
the run would **still** have burned 2.5 h building the dirty set before reaching
any of it. **Reopening FIX-1004 alone would not have saved this run.** It remains
worth doing; it is not the fix for FIX-1018.

**(C) Both, (A) dominating.** Ranked by measured contribution:

1. Dirty-set build outside the guard, `BitmapAnd` on a non-leading index column —
   ~9,290 s of a 9,367 s run.
2. Missing `to_type='official'` — 57% of recipients and 54% of dirty weight are
   unrollupable, and it put a PAC at the head of the cursor.
3. Monday's two concurrent 6 h EC rebuilds — the ~157× multiplier.
4. FR at 77.1% all-visible with zero vacuum owners (FIX-1013).
5. FIX-1003's arm vacuums never having run (0/12).

## 6. Recommendations (none executed — this session was read-only)

**Order matters; 1 and 2 are cheap and remove most of the cost.**

1. **Add `AND fr.to_type = 'official'` to both dirty-set branches** in
   `refresh_official_donor_rollup_incremental()`. Cuts the dirty set 2,890 → 1,250,
   removes 2.46 M of fabricated weight, and stops the cursor parking on PACs.
   One-line change; needs a watermark decision (see 6a).
2. **Add a partial index** matching the dirty-set predicate, e.g.
   `ON financial_relationships (updated_at) WHERE to_type='official' AND from_type='financial_entity' AND relationship_type IN ('donation','ie_support','ie_oppose')`.
   The `updated_at` leg alone already runs in **131 ms**; the aim is to make the
   planner take it without the 33.65 s `BitmapAnd` leg. Verify the plan flips
   before/after — adding `to_type='official'` on its own does **not** fix it
   (the planner just swaps in `financial_relationships_to`, another 4.17 M-entry
   scan). Weigh against playbook **C7**: FR takes zero HOT updates and already
   carries 16 indexes / 4,855 MB, so a *partial* index is the right shape.
3. **Bring the pre-loop work inside the budget.** Stamp elapsed after the
   dirty-set build and refuse to enter the loop if it already exceeds some
   fraction of the budget, logging `stop_reason: 'dirty_set_build_exhausted_budget'`.
   Today that cost is invisible in every log the procedure writes — the only
   reason it was findable is the `chunks`/`slowest_chunk_seconds`/`elapsed_seconds`
   triple disagreeing.
4. **Deconflict Monday.** `rebuild-ec-incremental-mon` (08:00) and
   `entity-connection-stats-rebuild` (11:00) both hit the 6 h axe and overlap the
   09:00 and 12:00 donor-rollup firings. Playbook **D3** — alter in place, by name.
   This overlaps FIX-969's scope.
5. **Fix the pg_cron startup timeouts (needs its own FIX).** 0/12 on the arm
   vacuums and 10/33 on donor-rollup is not incidental; it is a standing
   background-worker starvation condition that predates 08-10 (07-27, 08-04,
   08-05, 08-06, 08-08). Note the 08-10 14:12:47 pair — `odt-` and
   `treemap-individuals-vacuum-analyze` both launching at the same non-scheduled
   second — which is the launcher visibly behind. Start at `max_worker_processes`
   vs. the parallel workers the two 6 h rebuilds hold.
6. **Give `financial_relationships` a vacuum owner** (FIX-1013). It is the largest
   table on the box, at 77.1% AV, with 0 cron jobs naming it.
7. **Reap the three stranded `running` rows** — `entity_connections_rebuild`
   (08-10 08:00), `entity_connection_stats_rebuild` (08-10 11:00),
   `treemap_individuals_global_refresh` (08-10 22:35). All three backends died
   with the 01:08 restart. FIX-990.
8. **FIX-1004** stays worth doing, and after (1)–(3) it becomes the thing that
   actually drains the backlog. It is not the fix for this incident.
