# FIX-1185 — the `postgres` role's in-backend ceiling: census, arming proof, and the `transaction_timeout` read

cc-127, 2026-09-14 PDT (prod reads at 2026-09-15 00:36–00:46 UTC). Design of
record: `Claude/civitics/design-fix1185-postgres-role-ceiling-2026-09-14.md`,
decisions D1–D6.

**Verdict: the D3 gate FIRED. The 6 h → 3 h migration was not written and
nothing was pushed to prod.** The mechanism the design rests on is proven (§4)
and the `transaction_timeout` read is complete (§5); what did not survive
contact is the *number*, and the claim that the ceiling would sit strictly below
the watchdog. Both are §3.

Everything below is measured this session. Prod contact was READ-ONLY throughout
(`db-query.mjs --prod`, which wraps every statement in `SET TRANSACTION READ
ONLY`). The two experiments ran on the local prod-clone and were torn down
(§4, §5).

the census:

> Keep the cron command a PLAIN `CALL` (do NOT prefix `SET statement_timeout=…;`

The `0` group is exactly the escape hatch the design describes, and it is the
asymmetry that makes the whole design work: a supervised landing may raise or
remove its own bound as its own statement; a pg_cron job cannot, because its
command must stay single-statement.

The other **28 direct-pg files set no bound and inherit the role default.** They
are audits, investigations and verifiers that issue many short statements, and
the role default bounds each *statement*, not the script — so a script is only
at risk if one statement runs past the ceiling. The longest single ad-hoc
`postgres` statement measured in the pgss window is 1,832.4 s (0.51 h). **No
direct-pg script is known to run one statement past 3 h**, so read 4(c) forces
no `fix:add`.

## 3. What contradicted the design

### 3.1 The ceiling would not sit under the watchdog — for two active jobs it would sit *over* it

The design's posture is that "the watchdog stays PRIMARY" and the ceiling is a
backstop for the two cases the watchdog cannot cover. That holds only if every
per-job budget is below the ceiling. `public.cron_job_budget`, read on prod:

| jobname | budget_s | active | vs. a 10,800 s ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| rebuild-ec-incremental-mon | 18,000 | no | ceiling wins by 7,200 s |
| rebuild-ec-incremental | 18,000 | no | ceiling wins by 7,200 s |
| **donor-rollup-refresh** | **14,400** | **yes** | **ceiling fires 3,600 s first** |
| **contract-flow-rollups-refresh** | **10,800** | **yes** | **identical — ceiling always wins** |
| rule-taggers-daily / -weekly, financial-entity-totals-incremental | 7,200 | y / y / n | watchdog first |
| treemap-individuals-global-refresh, refresh-derived-mvs-daily / -weekly | 5,400 | yes | watchdog first |
| agency-staffing-rollup-refresh | 3,600 | yes | watchdog first |
| fe-crawl, group-donor-rollup-refresh, platform-counts-daily, donor-party-rollup-refresh, ec-crawl | 1,800 | yes | watchdog first |

For `donor-rollup-refresh` a 3 h ceiling becomes the operative bound an hour
before its own budget — the watchdog would never cancel it again. For
`contract-flow-rollups-refresh` the two numbers are **equal**, and because the
watchdog is measurably late by 4.8–973.9 s on a `*/2` cadence (design §0.3), the
ceiling would win every time. Two active jobs would silently move from "bounded
by a per-job budget that records what it did" to "bounded by a global timer that
records nothing job-specific" — the exact inversion of the design's stated
posture, and it is invisible in the design because neither job is in its §2
census table.

A 3 h ceiling is therefore not only a backstop; for those two it is a
**demotion of the watchdog**, and FIX-1109's `succeeded` + `'partial'` vs
`failed` distinction shifts with it.

### 3.2 `succeeded` is not `healthy`, and the census read it as if it were

`refresh-derived-mvs-daily` on 2026-08-31 ran **21,932.5 s and pg_cron recorded
`succeeded`**; the procedure's own row says `partial`. Any census that separates
healthy from incident on `cron.job_run_details.status` will mis-sort every C1
procedure, in both directions — which is how the design arrived at "every run
anyone would call healthy is ≤ 7,125.6 s" when five clean completions between
3.13 h and 5.16 h sit in the same table.

### 3.3 The census omitted the two jobs that decide the number

`donor-rollup-refresh` (largest active budget on prod, 14,400 s) and
`entity-connection-stats-rebuild` (longest clean completion on prod, 18,568.4 s,
and **no budget row at all**) are both absent from the design's §2 table. They
are the two rows that move the answer.

### 3.4 What did NOT contradict the design

The mechanism (§4), the permission (§4), `work_mem` survival (§4), the
`check:proconfig` posture (§6), the 6 h being a repo file (§1), and every pg_cron
job running as `postgres` (§1) all held exactly as written. The design's
reasoning is sound; its input table was incomplete.

## 4. Read 5 — a role-level default DOES arm a whole `CALL` across intra-procedure COMMITs

Measured on the local prod-clone (PG 17.6, same major and minor as prod) on the
**clone's live pg_cron scheduler**, which is the truer instrument and also the
only one available: `scripts/db-query.mjs --call` **always** prepends
`SET statement_timeout = '<timeout>'` (`db-query.mjs:184`) and exposes no flag to
suppress it, so a `--call` session can never carry the role default alone.

Probe `public._fix1185_probe_units(p_units, p_unit_seconds)` — cc-125's shape:
unit loop over `pg_sleep`, `EXCEPTION WHEN query_canceled` that records and sets
a flag, `COMMIT` per unit, `EXIT WHEN` the flag, terminal row.

**The permission** — run as `postgres` (`rolsuper = f`):

```
ALTER ROLE postgres IN DATABASE postgres SET statement_timeout = '5s';
→ ALTER ROLE                                     (no error)
→ setconfig now {statement_timeout=5s,work_mem=256MB}
```

An ordinary role may set its own database-scoped defaults. **`work_mem=256MB`
survived untouched**, which is the property the migration's `DO`-block read-back
was designed to assert.

**The arming** — `cron.schedule('_fix1185_probe','* * * * *','CALL public._fix1185_probe_units(5, 2);')`, jobid 78, verbatim:

```
 id |      at      |                             note
----+--------------+---------------------------------------------------------------
  1 | 00:43:00.042 | start statement_timeout=5s transaction_timeout=0 pid=1829
  2 | 00:43:02.053 | unit 1 ok
  3 | 00:43:04.114 | unit 2 ok
  4 | 00:43:05.042 | unit 3 CANCELED: canceling statement due to statement timeout
  5 | 00:43:05.049 | terminal canceled=t units_done=2
```

```
 jobid |          start_time           |           end_time            | dur_s |  status   | msg
-------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------+-----------+------
    78 | 2026-09-15 00:43:00.041091+00 | 2026-09-15 00:43:05.055002+00 | 5.014 | succeeded | CALL
```

Every predicted property holds: the fresh pg_cron session inherited `5s` from
the role default with no `SET` of its own; two units committed; **the third was
cancelled at 5.000 s — across two intervening COMMITs**; the record-and-STOP
handler ran; the terminal row was written; no further units ran; and **the CALL
RETURNED** rather than erroring out, so pg_cron recorded `succeeded`. A second
firing at 00:44:00 reproduced it identically (5.014 s).

Restored to `'6h'` and read back: `{statement_timeout=6h,work_mem=256MB}`.

**This is the design's load-bearing measurement and it passes.** The mechanism
is sound at any number; only the number is unsettled.

## 5. Read 6 — `transaction_timeout` (D5, measurement only; nothing built)

Same probe, same scheduler, `statement_timeout` restored to `6h` first so the
only variable is `transaction_timeout`, set as a role default.

**Part 1 — does it re-arm per unit?** `transaction_timeout = '3s'`, units of
**2 s** (jobid 79):

```
 id |      at      |                            note
----+--------------+------------------------------------------------------------
 11 | 00:45:00.018 | start statement_timeout=6h transaction_timeout=3s pid=1934
 12 | 00:45:02.026 | unit 1 ok
 13 | 00:45:04.031 | unit 2 ok
 14 | 00:45:06.037 | unit 3 ok
 15 | 00:45:08.042 | unit 4 ok
 16 | 00:45:10.048 | unit 5 ok
 17 | 00:45:10.053 | terminal canceled=f units_done=5
```
```
 jobid | dur_s  |  status   | msg
    79 | 10.039 | succeeded | CALL
```

**It re-arms per unit.** All five units ran and the whole CALL took 10.039 s
under a 3 s bound — so it is armed at TRANSACTION start and disarmed at COMMIT,
exactly as PG 17 documents, and it does **not** bound the CALL as a whole. This
is the branch the design hoped for: a genuine in-backend **per-unit** bound with
no extra backend.

**Part 2 — what does the session get when a unit exceeds it?**
`transaction_timeout = '3s'`, units of **4 s** (jobid 80):

```
 id |      at      |                            note
----+--------------+------------------------------------------------------------
 18 | 00:46:00.036 | start statement_timeout=6h transaction_timeout=3s pid=1995
(1 row)
```
```
 start_time                    | end_time                      | dur_s | status | msg
 2026-09-15 00:46:00.034778+00 | 2026-09-15 00:46:03.047321+00 | 3.013 | failed | server closed the connection unexpectedly
                               |                               |       |        |   This probably means the server terminated abnor…
```

**It TERMINATES the backend.** No unit row, no terminal row, no `query_canceled`
— the `EXCEPTION WHEN query_canceled` handler never ran, and pg_cron recorded
`failed` with a *connection-loss* message rather than a timeout message.

**The one-line answer for FIX-1186:** a role-level `transaction_timeout` on PG
17.6 re-arms per unit (5 × 2 s units all completed, CALL 10.039 s, pg_cron
`succeeded`), so a per-unit in-backend bound needs no extra backend — but when a
unit exceeds it the backend is TERMINATED, not cancelled: the record-and-STOP
handler never runs, no terminal row is written, and `cron.job_run_details` reads
`failed` / "server closed the connection unexpectedly", so FIX-1109's
`succeeded` + `'partial'` signal is lost and the terminal row would have to come
from a reaper instead of the handler.

**Teardown.** `ALTER ROLE postgres IN DATABASE postgres RESET transaction_timeout`
(RESET is correct — it was never set), both probe jobs unscheduled, procedure and
log table dropped. Verified clean: 0 probe procs, 0 probe tables, 0 probe jobs,
`{statement_timeout=6h,work_mem=256MB}`.

## 7. What the next prompt needs to decide

The gate stops the number, not the design. Three ways forward, in the order the
evidence supports:

1. **Re-decide D2 against a stated window.** If the basis is the trailing 30
   days, the healthy max is ~7,201.7 s and 3 h stands with a 50 % margin — but
   the decision then has to say so, and has to say what happens when
   `entity-connection-stats-rebuild` (no budget row, 18,568.4 s clean) is
   re-enabled. If the basis is the retained window, the healthy max is
   18,568.4 s and the ceiling cannot be 3 h.
2. **Settle the watchdog inversion first (§3.1).** A ceiling at or below an
   active job's budget silently demotes the watchdog for that job. Either the
   ceiling sits above every active budget (today that means above 14,400 s, i.e.
   past 4 h), or `donor-rollup-refresh`'s and `contract-flow-rollups-refresh`'s
   budgets come down with it, deliberately and in the same change.
3. **Give `entity-connection-stats-rebuild` a `cron_job_budget` row** regardless
   of what the ceiling becomes. It is the longest clean completion on prod and
   the only thing that has ever bounded it is the role default.

`transaction_timeout` (§5) is a measurement, not a proposal; it belongs to
FIX-1186 and nothing here is built on it.
