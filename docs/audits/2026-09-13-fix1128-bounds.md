# FIX-1128 — claimed vs real vs observed bounds, on prod

Companion to [`2026-09-13-fix1128-bounds.tsv`](2026-09-13-fix1128-bounds.tsv).
All 91 census functions, measured 2026-09-13 (UTC).

Three numbers per function:

- **claimed** — the `statement_timeout` proconfig value the strip removed.
- **real** — what actually bounds a call, from prod's role configuration plus
  the probe below.
- **observed** — from the instrument appropriate to that function's class.

## Prod role configuration (read-only)

```
anon                   {statement_timeout=3s}
authenticated          {statement_timeout=8s}
authenticator          {session_preload_libraries=safeupdate,statement_timeout=8s,lock_timeout=8s}
service_role           (none)
postgres               {"search_path=..."}          ← rolconfig only
```

`postgres`'s timeout is **not** in `rolconfig`. It is database-scoped, in
`pg_db_role_setting` for `(db=postgres, role=postgres)`:
`{statement_timeout=6h, work_mem=256MB}`. That 6 h is the ceiling every pg_cron
`CALL` runs under, and it is the number behind FIX-1123's 21,638 s.

## The `service_role` probe — class B's real bound, measured

`service_role` has no `rolconfig`, so whether an impersonated `service_role`
inherits `authenticator`'s 8 s was a measurement, not a deduction. Via
`createAdminClient()` → PostgREST → `.rpc('_fix1128_probe_sleep', …)`:

| `p_seconds` | result | wall |
|---:|---|---:|
| 7 | **OK**, `data=1` | 7,426 ms |
| 9 | `57014 canceling statement due to statement timeout` | 8,212 ms |
| 10 | `57014` | 8,174 ms |
| 10 | `57014` | 8,080 ms |
| 2 (control) | **OK**, `data=1` | 2,246 ms |

**Class B's real bound is 8 s.** The impersonated `service_role` does inherit
`authenticator`'s session value across `SET ROLE`.

### A methodological finding that changes how the observed column reads

The cancelled 10 s probe left **no row in `pg_stat_statements`**; the 2 s
control did. So pgss's `max_exec_time` is a maximum over statements that
**completed**. Two consequences:

- An `EXCEEDS real` verdict is strong evidence — the statement really ran that
  long and finished.
- A `within` verdict proves nothing about cancellations. Every 57014 is
  invisible here and has to be read from the Logs API instead.

## The four `EXCEEDS real`

| function | class | claimed | real | observed max | filed |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| `get_official_page` | A | 5 s | **3 s** (anon) | 4,608.0 ms | **FIX-1180** |
| `link_officials_to_districts` | B | 5 min | **8 s** | 12,402.8 ms | **FIX-1181** |
| `refresh_primary_source_for_entities` | B | 60 s | **8 s** | 9,835.7 ms | **FIX-1181** |
| `rebuild_entity_search_index` | C1 | 1200 s | **6 h** | 21,638,300 ms | already FIX-1123; Half 2 |

`get_official_page` is granted to **both** `anon` and `authenticated`, so it
runs under two different real bounds — 3 s and 8 s. A 4,608 ms completion is an
authenticated call; the same work from an anon visitor is cancelled, and
(per the methodological note) leaves no pgss row to see it by. 578 calls, mean
340.5 ms, stddev 913.1 ms — the tail is 2.7× the mean, so it is not rare.

The two class-B rows are filed as **one** item rather than one per function,
against D4's letter: they are the same phenomenon, not two slow functions. Both
completed past a bound that was directly measured at 8 s minutes earlier, and
the proconfig is excluded as the cause (a looser proconfig cannot extend a
tighter session bound — measured on the clone: 2 s session, 30 s proconfig,
cancelled at 2.001 s, identical to the same body with none). The open candidate
is a pooled `authenticator` connection carrying a non-`LOCAL`
`SET statement_timeout`.

`rebuild_entity_search_index` is the FIX-1123 run: 21,638.3 s against a 1200 s
claim and a 6 h ceiling — 18× its claimed value, stopped only by the role
setting. It is the single clearest demonstration of why the decoration was worth
removing, and it is Half 2's problem, not a new filing.

## `get_proposal_counts_by_agency` — the design's first suspect, cleared

The design named it as the one to look at first: 60 s claimed, 3 s/8 s real,
public grant. Measured: **2 calls, mean 751.7 ms, max 1,051.2 ms** — well inside
the anon bound. Nothing to do.

## The pattern worth keeping in view

19 of 91 carried a claim **looser** than the bound they actually run under —
`rebuild_entity_connections` claimed 60 min against a real 8 s, and
`refresh_jurisdiction_page_cache` / `refresh_official_content_ids` claimed
600 s. Those are the ones that read as permission, and they are the reason the
strip is worth more than the tidiness: not one of them ever had it.
